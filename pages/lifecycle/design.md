Use your [training plan](plan.md) to draft a syllabus for the course and a lesson plan for each session. The syllabus gives learners an overview of the topics, prerequisites and learning outcomes. The lesson plan helps trainers decide what learners will do, how much time each activity needs and how to check progress. In the [Develop](develop.md) stage, you will prepare the materials for those activities. Revisit the training plan as needed while you refine the design.

## What are the pedagogical aspects?

1. **Refine the [learning outcomes](https://elixir-europe-training.github.io/ELIXIR-TrP-GOBLET-Train-the-Trainer/session_2#session-2---part-ii---learning-outcomes-three-frameworks).** Describe what learners will be able to do by the end of the session. Use observable verbs such as *compare*, *interpret* or *create*. Bloom’s taxonomy can help you choose a verb that matches the level of learning you expect.
2. **Choose learning activities.** Give learners a chance to practise the task described in each learning outcome. Choose activities and a delivery format that suit the topic, the learners’ prior knowledge and the time available.
3. **Decide how to check learning.** For each outcome, decide what learners could say, do or produce to show they have achieved it. A short exercise, discussion prompt or quiz can help you check progress during the session. If the course offers certification, define the criteria and how learners will demonstrate that they meet them.

### Example from a hands-on bioinformatics workshop

- **Learning outcome.** By the end of the session, learners can identify which samples cluster together in a principal component analysis (PCA) plot and describe how the clusters relate to the sample groups.
- **Activity.** Give pairs a plot with samples labelled by group. Ask them to mark the clusters and compare their interpretations.
- **Check.** Give learners a different plot and ask them to identify the clusters independently and explain whether they match the sample groups.

### Curriculum and learning models

**Nicholls’ model** links five phases of curriculum design, from defining learning outcomes and choosing activities and content to planning assessment and evaluating the course. Use the model to check how the phases fit together and decide when to revisit earlier choices. In the PCA example, if the outcome is to explain how clusters relate to sample groups, make sure learners practise giving that explanation and then do it independently on a new plot. See [Session 2 of the ELIXIR-GOBLET Train-the-Trainer programme](https://elixir-europe-training.github.io/ELIXIR-TrP-GOBLET-Train-the-Trainer/session_2#session-2---part-iv---backward-instructional-design) for more on backward design.

**Döring’s model** alternates between processing new information and recalling and applying it. In the PCA example, plan a short demonstration of how you interpret a plot, then give pairs a different plot to interpret and ask them to explain their reasoning.

## What are the logistical aspects?

- Use the [syllabus guidance](https://elixir-europe-training.github.io/ELIXIR-TrP-GOBLET-Train-the-Trainer/session_2#session-2---part-iii---syllabus-and-learning-experiences) and [lesson plan guidance](https://elixir-europe-training.github.io/ELIXIR-TrP-GOBLET-Train-the-Trainer/lesson_plan) to document your design. If your training is part of a wider programme, use [Learning Paths](../resources/learning-paths.md) to help sequence the lessons.
- Find materials you can reuse in [TeSS](https://tess.elixir-europe.org/) or [Glittr.org](https://glittr.org). Choose materials that support your learning outcomes and use formats that make them easier to share and reuse. See the [FAIR Training Handbook](../resources/fair-training-handbook.md) for guidance.
- List any gaps to address in the [Develop](develop.md) stage.
